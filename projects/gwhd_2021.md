## Overview

**Personal Interests:** i) Explore SOTA vision foundation models (DINOv3) on a second task. ii)
Applied to a domain new to me: wheat heads in dense field vegetation. iii) Claude-assisted rapid
prototyping.

**Toolchain:** GitHub · HuggingFace (DINOv3 weights) · Weights & Biases · RunPod (1× RTX PRO 4500
Blackwell, ~$0.72/hr)

**Dataset:** [GWHD 2021](https://www.global-wheat.com/gwhd.html) — 6,515 images at 1024×1024,
275,468 wheat-head boxes, one class, collected across 47 domains (site × session) on five continents.

> **Dataset Limitations.** GWHD is a small academic dataset. Useful for a personal project, but not
> enough diversity to ship a product. The image views are fairly zoomed-in, and it's unclear if
> these are representative of the view from a moving tractor.

The **official splits are disjoint by domain** — zero overlap between train,
val and test. Val and test also come from different continents than train.

*A typical image from one domain per split, with its ground-truth boxes: dense Swiss wheat in

## 1. Questions

The [RELLIS-3D project]({{ '/projects/rellis3d/' | relative_url }}) found that multi-scale
stride-4 DINOv3 features, the densest setting, beat stride-16 for segmentation. How does the feature stride affect detection?

We might expect detection to amplify the effect. GWHD's test boxes have a median side of **48 px**, and the
smallest 5% are under **21 px**. At stride 16, a 21 px box is 1.3 feature cells. A CenterNet-style
detector localizes by picking peaks on its output grid, so the stride is the localization
resolution, not just the feature resolution.

The backbone stays frozen throughout. Everything below shares one training loop, one input size (1024×1024, native resolution), and one training schedule.

| # | question | arms | matched on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does a finer stride help? | ViT-S+/16, ConvNeXt-T s16, ConvNeXt-T s4 | schedule, head, input size |
| 2 | What does the head cost? | linear 1×1 head vs small conv head | backbone, schedule |

Can we get away with a 1×1 head on top of the frozen features? Or do we need spatial context after the frozen layers?

## 2. The dataset

| split | images | boxes | domains | countries | median boxes/img |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| train | 3,657 | 163,690 | 18 | 5 | 45 |
| val | 1,476 | 44,347 | 11 | 4 | 28 |
| test | 1,382 | 67,431 | 18 | 6 | 46 |

The split is geographic: **Europe → the rest of the world.** Train is entirely European (France,
Switzerland, UK, Norway, Belgium); val is Japan, Australia, Canada and China; test is the US,
Australia, Mexico, China, Japan and Sudan.

A few things about the data shaped the work:

- **Train is narrower than "18 domains" suggests.** One domain (`ETHZ_1`) holds 30% of all
  training boxes, and France is 13 of the 18 domains.
- **Test boxes are ~35% smaller than train.** Median side: 73 px train, 88 px val, **48 px** test.
  The scale shift runs the same direction as the domain shift, so a coarse-stride model is
  penalized twice on the test set.
- **Density varies 50× across domains**, from 2.4 to 122.7 boxes per image, with 190 in the
  densest test image. The peak-extraction budget is 256 per image; CenterNet's default of 128
  would silently truncate the hardest images in the hardest split.
- **1,537 of the `.png` files are actually JPEG**, and the mislabelling correlates with the split:
  82% of test, 11% of train, 0% of val. JPEG artifacts are then a real domain shift aligned with
  the hardest split, so the true format is recorded per image as a covariate.

## 3. Detection: a head inspired by CenterNet, on frozen DINOv3

The head predicts three maps on the backbone's output grid: a center heatmap, a box size, and a sub-cell offset. There's only one category (`wheat_head`), so there is no classification branch and mAP
reduces to single-class AP.

| head | structure | receptive field |
|---|---|---|
| `linear` | one 1×1 conv per output branch | a single cell |
| `conv` | small 3×3 stem (width 64), then the three branches | local neighborhood |

Decoding takes local maxima of the heatmap with a 2D max-pool (in place of box NMS), keeps the top
256, and reads size and offset at each peak.

**Metric:** COCO AP@[.5:.95], AP50 and AP_small — pooled, and per-domain, with a minimum-support floor.

## 4. Results

### Experiment 1: does stride dominate?

Conv head, 8 epochs, best checkpoint chosen on val and scored **once** on test. All three arms
share the final pipeline: pixel-sized Gaussians, fp32 head, and a 7×7 peak window at stride 4
(3×3 at stride 16).

**Validation** (11 domains, 1,476 images): used to pick each arm's best checkpoint.

| arm | stride | head params | best step | AP | AP50 | AP75 | AP_small | AP_medium | AP_large | AR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ConvNeXt-T, 4 stages | 4 | 830k | 4,575 | 0.253 | 0.726 | 0.094 | **0.040** | 0.236 | 0.302 | 0.358 |
| ConvNeXt-T, stages 2–3 | 16 | 664k | 6,405 | **0.277** | **0.753** | **0.120** | 0.020 | **0.252** | **0.330** | **0.378** |
| ViT-S+/16, last 4 blocks | 16 | 885k | 6,405 | 0.267 | 0.744 | 0.105 | 0.034 | 0.251 | 0.305 | 0.366 |

**Test** (18 held-out domains, 1,382 images): each best checkpoint scored once.

| arm | stride | AP | AP50 | AP75 | AP_small | AP_medium | AP_large | AR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ConvNeXt-T, 4 stages | 4 | **0.131** | **0.455** | 0.028 | 0.030 | **0.152** | 0.149 | **0.227** |
| ConvNeXt-T, stages 2–3 | 16 | **0.131** | 0.442 | **0.036** | 0.020 | 0.150 | **0.186** | 0.221 |
| ViT-S+/16, last 4 blocks | 16 | 0.125 | 0.445 | 0.026 | **0.035** | 0.143 | 0.140 | 0.212 |

*AP_small, AP_medium and AP_large use COCO's size bands: boxes under 32 px on a side, 32–96 px,
and over 96 px.*

On the test set, stride does not separate these detectors. Stride 16 leads on the validation set by 0.024 AP; on test the stride-4 and stride-16 ConvNeXt arms tie at **0.131**, with stride 4 slightly ahead on AP50.
Against the stride-16 ConvNeXt, the stride-4 ConvNeXt wins on small heads and loses on large
heads and AP75, on both val and test. Medium heads are close: stride 16 leads on val (0.252 vs
0.236), and the two are level on test (0.152 vs 0.150). Every arm roughly halves from val to test, the domain shift the official
split is built to measure.

The "penalized twice" prediction from §2 (smaller boxes *and* new domains on test) did not
materialize for stride 16: the remaining test differences are small.

![validation AP per epoch]({{ '/assets/gwhd_2021/val_ap_curves.png' | relative_url }})

The dashed line is the same stride-4 arm before the three fixes in §5. Together they moved it
from **0.199 to 0.253** val AP, closing two-thirds of its gap to stride 16. Stride 16 barely moved
(0.272 → 0.277): at stride 16, the Gaussian for a median head was about one output cell wide
under both rules, so its target hardly changed.

### Experiment 2: what does the head cost?

| backbone | stride | linear 1×1 (params) | conv 3×3 stem (params) | Δ val AP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ConvNeXt-T | 4 | 0.136 (7.2k) | 0.199 (830k) | +0.063 |
| ConvNeXt-T | 16 | 0.152 (5.8k) | 0.272 (664k) | +0.120 |
| ViT-S+/16 | 16 | 0.131 (7.7k) | 0.251 (885k) | +0.120 |

Validation AP for matched pairs, from the original pipeline (CornerNet radius, bf16 head, 3×3
peak window everywhere), before the fixes in §5. We didn't re-run the linear heads on the final
pipeline, so these results show the direction of the effect but aren't directly comparable to
Experiment 1.

Frozen DINOv3 features are not linearly decodable into centers. A single 3×3 layer roughly
doubled AP at stride 16. Peak extraction needs local context.

### Per domain, the backbone matters more than the stride

![test AP per held-out domain]({{ '/assets/gwhd_2021/test_ap_per_domain.png' | relative_url }})

We report per-domain AP for the 18 domains in the test set.

The two ConvNeXt arms track each other, but the ViT backbone diverges in both directions.

- On the hardest domains (Terraref in the US, the UQ sessions in Australia) the ViT leads, up to
  **0.089 vs 0.049** on UQ_11.
- On all four KSU domains (US) it trails both ConvNeXt arms, down to **0.082 vs 0.146** on KSU_4.

The ViT is best on 9 domains, stride 4 on 6, stride 16 on 3. Mean per-domain AP is a tie:
0.129 (ConvNeXt-T stride 4), 0.130 (ConvNeXt-T stride 16) and 0.126 (ViT-S+/16).

### What the detections look like

![ground truth and detections from each arm on four val frames]({{ '/assets/gwhd_2021/detections_val.jpg' | relative_url }})

Four val frames from three domains, at each arm's best checkpoint, with boxes drawn above score
0.3. The same pattern holds in every row. At that fixed threshold stride 4 under-fires (11–22
boxes for 20–39 heads) and misses the large elongated heads. Both stride-16 arms stack duplicate
boxes on those same wheat heads, most visibly in the densest frame (52 and 63 boxes for 39 heads). A
ranking metric like AP mostly ignores this calibration gap, but a deployed detector with a fixed
threshold would not.

![ground truth and detections from each arm on three test domains]({{ '/assets/gwhd_2021/detections_test.jpg' | relative_url }})

The same comparison on test, one median-density image from each of three held-out domains: UQ_11
(the ViT's biggest win), KSU_4 (its biggest loss) and NAU_3 (the easiest domain for every arm).
Stride 4's under-firing carries over to test. On KSU_4, whose heads are large and overlap heavily,
both stride-16 arms pile boxes onto the same heads. These test panels were rendered on CPU in
fp32, so scores near the 0.3 cutoff can differ slightly from the bf16 GPU runs.

*One seed per arm. Differences under ~0.02 AP are within the epoch-to-epoch noise in the curves
above.*

## 5. Learnings

Each of these produced a plausible-looking loss curve, and each showed up only in AP.

- **Size the heatmap Gaussian in pixels, not grid cells.** CornerNet's radius rule is a tolerance
  on how far the center may drift, floored to whole cells. On GWHD it floored to **zero** for every
  box at stride 16, and to 1–2 cells at stride 4. That left a 70 px head with one positive cell
  and ~300 full-weight negatives *inside the object*, whose features are nearly identical to the
  center's. The head's best compromise was to score everything low. Sizing the Gaussian from the
  box in pixels (`σ = 0.54 · side / 6`, after TTFNet) gave both strides the same physical target.

  **Pixel units helped the centers, but not the box sizes.** The head also predicts each box's
  width and height, in grid cells, so the same wheat head is a 4× larger number at stride 4 than
  at stride 16. Measuring size as a fraction of the image instead removes that stride-dependence,
  but it made sizes less precise: on the stride-16 model, box-size error doubled (8.2 → 16.0 px)
  and AP fell from 0.278 to 0.262. We kept grid units, which leaves a **known limitation for the
  stride-4 model**: its size outputs don't reach the larger values within the training budget,
  so it does well on small boxes and poorly large ones.
- **Max-pool NMS kernel size is a per-stride setting.** A 3×3 pool covers 48 px at stride 16 but
  only 12 px at stride 4, so neighboring cells on the same head survive as duplicate detections.
  At stride 4 (linear head), widening the kernel from 3 to 5 to 7 raised validation AP from **0.149 to 0.158 to
  0.160**. But widening the kernel *lost* AP at stride 16.
- **Keep bf16 autocast in the trunk, and run the head in fp32.** In bf16, 31% of stride-4 peaks
  above 0.1 were exact ties with a neighbor and survived peak NMS as duplicates, compared with 1
  in 92k in fp32. Moving only the head to fp32 was worth **+0.013 AP / +0.047 AP50** on the
  stride-4 linear head. The head is a few small convs, so it costs almost nothing.
